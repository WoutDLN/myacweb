## Abstract

Almost simultaneously, two on-line anglophone lexicons related to textual scholarship have been
created, with a different focus, but emerging from the same awareness that textual scholars, working
on different periods, from Antiquity to the 21st century, and anchored in different traditions of
scholarship, very often do not understand each other, because they use the same technical words
with different meanings. The two lexicons we are presenting here aim at remedying this cacophony.

1. [Lexicon of Scholarly Editing](https://lexiconse.uantwerpen.be)[^1] (LSE), edited by Wout Dillen and Dirk Van Hulle:
398 entries, 760 definitions, quoted from 87 sources in their original language (English, French,
German, Italian).

2. [Parvum lexicon stemmatologicum](https://wiki.hiit.fi/display/stemmatology/Parvum+lexicon+stemmatologicum) (PLS), edited by Odd Einar Haugen (2011-2015), Philipp Roelli
and Caroline Macé: 
242 entries, from 17 contributors, in English, with translations of each term in French, German,
Italian, and occasionally Latin.

LSE and PLS are different in their conception and in their scope, but they are complementary and
several entries are present in both lexicons (for example: “reading”, “apparatus”, “archetype”,
“stemma” etc.). 

In their present form, these two lexicons are already very useful and valuable, yet we would like to
go a step further and, building upon the material and experience we have gained in the process, we
consider preparing a glossary and/or dictionary of textual scholarship. The paper will address the
problems and complications involved in this enterprise.
